Tree Removal

Big, small, leaning, or long gone. Taken down safely, piece by piece.

Most trees we remove don't fall. They come down in pieces, in order, each one on a rope. A climber goes up, works the crown down limb by limb, and the ground crew lowers every piece into a clear zone, unclips it, and drags it to the pile. On a tight lot with a fence, a garden, and a service line, that is the whole job: control.

When removal is the right call

  • The tree is dead or mostly dead. Dead wood is brittle, and it only gets more dangerous the longer it stands.
  • A storm split the trunk or tore out a leader, and what's left can't be saved with pruning.
  • It's leaning at a house, garage, or driveway, and the lean is new.
  • The roots are heaving the driveway, the sewer line keeps clogging, or the tree simply outgrew the lot it was planted on fifty years ago.
  • It's an ash. The emerald ash borer killed most of the ash in Erie County, and standing dead ash sheds limbs without warning.

Not sure it needs to go? Say so. Plenty of trees people assume are done can be saved with a hard prune, and we'd rather tell you that at the estimate than sell you a removal.

How it works in a real yard

We look at the tree, the targets under it, and the access. If there's room, some limbs get dropped free into a clear zone. When there isn't, and in most Buffalo yards there isn't, everything gets rigged: a rope on the piece, a cut, a controlled ride down. The trunk comes down last, in rounds. Nothing lands where it wasn't sent.

What happens to the wood

Limbs go through the chipper. Trunk wood gets hauled unless you want it. If you have a stove, we'll cut the rounds to stove length and stack them where you say. Chips are free if you want them for beds. The stump stays unless you add stump grinding, and most people do while the crew is already there.

What moves the price

  • Size and height, and how much of the crown overhangs things that matter.
  • Access. A backyard tree behind a narrow gate takes longer than a front-yard tree beside the driveway.
  • Condition. Dead trees are slower and more careful work than live ones.
  • What you want left: hauled clean, or wood stacked and chips dropped.

You get the number in writing after a free look, and the number is the price. No fuel surprise, no cleanup line item that appears at the end. When the last load leaves, the lawn gets raked, and then you walk it before you pay.
