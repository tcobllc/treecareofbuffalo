Land Clearing

Lots, fence lines, and overgrown corners opened back up.

Every neighborhood has one: the back quarter of a lot that went wild a decade ago and now nobody goes back there. Buckthorn, box elder, wild grape strangling a fence, a few volunteer trees that got serious about it. Clearing is the job of taking that from jungle back to ground you can use.

What we clear

  • Fence lines. Trees grown into and through fencing, cut out without wrecking what's left of the fence, and the line opened up so a new fence can actually go in.
  • Overgrown corners and back lots. Brush cut, saplings dropped, everything chipped on site.
  • Building and garage sites. Trees down, stumps ground, the footprint left clean for the contractor.
  • View and light corridors. Sometimes clearing is three ugly volunteers and a wall of brush, and the yard doubles in size.

What happens to all of it

Brush and limbs go through the chipper, and the chips either leave with us or get spread where you want them, which on a cleared lot is often the honest answer for mud control. Trunk wood is hauled or stacked. Stumps get ground if the ground is staying lawn, or left low if a machine is coming to dig anyway. Say what the land is for and we finish it to match.

What we don't do

We're a tree crew, not an excavator. Grading, digging, and hauling soil are someone else's trade, and we'll say so instead of doing it badly. What we hand your contractor is a lot with nothing woody standing on it.

Two things worth knowing

  • If the clearing runs along a property line, be sure where the line is before we cut. A survey stake costs less than a neighbor dispute over a tree that turned out to be theirs.
  • Winter is a fine time to clear. Frozen ground carries equipment without rutting the lawn, the brush is bare and easy to work, and the calendar is easier to book.

Scope drives price, so this one always starts with a walk. We look, we talk through what stays and what goes, and the number shows up in writing. The number is the price.
